# Smart Safety Tag V1 — Design Decisions

The editable KiCad design contains a 60 × 42 mm tag PCB and a 32 × 22 mm charging-adapter PCB. Both boards use two copper layers and 1.0 mm FR-4. The phone supplies location and Internet access. The tag provides BLE, audio capture, motion sensing, a physical SOS button, battery monitoring, RGB status and vibration.

The exact passive order codes, voltage ratings and purchasing quantities are in the generated BOMs. Resistors are 1% Yageo RC0603 parts; capacitors are the listed TDK parts. Purchasing availability can change, so substitute parts require another voltage, capacitance, package and layout review.

C13 uses a 22 µF/16 V 1206 C3216X5R1C226M160AB. It replaces the initial 10 µF/25 V 0805 input capacitor after its manufacturer DC-bias curve showed insufficient derating margin near the charger's 4.5 V SYS rail. The larger capacitor's footprint fits the regulator area. The numerical allowance and remaining physical qualification are recorded in [power-budget.md](power-budget.md).

The battery-temperature circuit uses RT1 = B57332V5103A260, a 10 kΩ B3435 NTC, on the rear PCB face. **RT1 must be thermally bonded to the cell using an insulating interface that distributes pressure.** Its placement inside the battery envelope does not establish thermal contact. A TLV7042 window pulls CHG_OK low on a temperature fault, turns Q1 off and lets R16 drive charger /CE high. GPIO16 can also inhibit charging through Q2; it cannot override a hardware fault. The modeled corner check covers resistor/NTC tolerances, charger bias, supply variation, comparator offset and hysteresis. Open/short fault response and thermal lag still require measurements. [Comparator datasheet](https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/tlv7032.pdf).

The module antenna projects 3.75 mm beyond the board's top edge. Its all-layer copper, track, via and component keepout remains in the PCB. Keep the battery, metal clip and magnets outside that region. The bottom-port microphone uses the specified 0.4 mm unplated acoustic hole. Its enclosure needs an aligned acoustic opening and a suitable seal. The selected battery outline, lead exit, button travel, LED view, motor support, cell insulation and pogo compression must be checked in a physical enclosure mockup before freezing a wearable product.

The hardware supports continuous PDM capture while the MCU is awake. Keyword inference, phone emergency messaging and product firmware are separate from the included hardware-test application. Continuous keyword detection is **not available in deep sleep** in this design.
